# Develop or review a school route and crossing plan

**Capability ID** — `cap.virginia-mutcd.school-area`

## R — Source

§7A.02 and Figure 7A-1, PDF pp. 855–857; Chapters 7B–7D, PDF pp. 858–872; §4C.06 for signal Warrant 5.

## I — Interpretation

A coordinated school route plan maps the existing network and established crossing points. The traffic control plan builds from actual route and crossing needs. Each speed zone, warning assembly, marking or supervision treatment has its own applicable provisions.

## A1 — Examples

Source illustration. Figure 7A-1, PDF p. 855, shows a school map with pedestrian routes and crossing/control symbols.

Synthetic exercise. The school and traffic officials provide a base map and two proposed crossings, with no gap study or established crossing records. Draft the map layers and flag crossing treatment and guard decisions for field confirmation.

## A2 — When to use

Build or audit a Virginia school walk-route map, crossing plan, school sign and speed zone review, or adult crossing guard checklist.

Use pedestrian crossing for a detailed treatment screen and signal warrants for Warrant 5.

## E — Execution

**Inputs** — School and approaches, routes and attendance area information, crossings and existing controls, roadway geometry and speed, student crossing data, gaps and sight distance, agency roles and restrictions, and hours in effect.

1. Inventory schools and walking approaches with school, law enforcement and traffic officials. Map streets, school, existing devices, established walk routes and crossings. Mark proposed routes distinctly in the plan.
2. Inspect crossings using §7A.02, including route continuity, walking distance, traffic and visibility. Record missing observations and compare candidate locations using common criteria.
3. Apply Chapter 7B for school area, crossing, speed limit or other treatments. For an established reduced speed zone, document its engineering or statutory basis and check §7B.05 advance S1-1, start and end signing, assembly and beacon conditions. The static assembly is limited to portable or tilt-over use. Use Chapter 7C for markings, 7D for adult guards and Part 4 when signal evaluation is requested.
4. Deliver the map or structured map-ready layer list, a crossing matrix, the referenced device selections or gaps, and coordination needed for implementation.

**Deliverable** — School route plan, crossing review, and a responsibility/action list.

## B — Boundaries

A school address and points on a map provide a starting inventory. Device choice and control hours depend on field evidence and responsible authority records.
