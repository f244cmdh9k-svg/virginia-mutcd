# Prepare a grade-crossing diagnostic review

**Capability ID** — `cap.virginia-mutcd.grade-crossing`

## R — Source

§§8A.05–8A.08, PDF pp. 875–877; Chapters 8B–8E; §§8D.09–8D.12, PDF pp. 910–918; §4F.19 and §4C.10 for adjacent-signal issues.

## I — Interpretation

The appropriate grade-crossing control system is determined through the Diagnostic Team engineering study. Minimum track clearance distance and clear storage distance answer different questions. Coordinate highway, rail/transit and regulatory decisions where applicable.

## A1 — Examples

Source illustration. Figure 8D-2 shows an example four-quadrant gate system at a two-lane highway, PDF pp. 907–908.

Synthetic exercise. On a single perpendicular crossing with no exit gate or adjacent crossing, the upstream edge of the entrance gate arm is station 0 ft. The far rail is station 30 ft and the downstream stop line is station 116 ft, with the intersection farther than the 6 ft rail offset. The track clearance interval extends to station 36 ft and the lane has 80 ft of clear storage (§8A.07; station distances are illustrative).

## A2 — When to use

Screen a Virginia highway-rail or light-rail crossing, calculate track clearance and available storage, or prepare a Diagnostic Team or rail preemption issue list.

Use signal warrants for intersection Warrant 9 and signal design for the associated highway signal plan.

## E — Execution

**Inputs** — Crossing and rail/transit operator, jurisdiction and control type, track and roadway geometry, gate/line locations, lane and queue observations, nearby controls, sight distance, train and road-user data and any existing interconnection.

1. Convene or identify the Diagnostic Team under §8A.05 and record participating highway, rail/transit and regulatory entities. Classify highway-rail vs. LRT, active vs. passive, and vehicle and pathway users. Document sight distance, geometry, demand, queues and crash/operation factors.
2. Under §8A.07, use the gate arm, stop line, or defined unpaved-road offset as applicable for the upstream endpoint. Find the applicable downstream endpoint using the far rail, intersection edge, or exit gate. Use the longer qualifying center- or edge-line measurement for skew. Apply §8A.08 for adjacent crossings and its relevant facts.
3. Measure lane-specific clear storage from the downstream track-clearance endpoint to the next normal stopping point. Compare to expected queued vehicles and flag track-occupancy risk. Preserve the two distances separately with a sketch or measured endpoints.
4. Screen passive signs and assemblies, markings, active systems, and pathway controls under 8B–8E. Screen §8D.09 ¶¶03–07 Guidance for the 200 ft and beyond-200 ft cases, using the control and warning-system types and any Diagnostic Team determination. Apply the separate ¶13 Standard where flashing-light signals exist and any highway traffic signal face is within 50 ft of any rail. Verify queuing and warning/timing inputs with the team.
5. Return a diagnostic memo with survey inputs, calculations, device and coordination issues, owners and next verification.

**Deliverable** — Crossing diagnostic record with traceable distances and preliminary preemption/coordination findings.

## B — Boundaries

Timing and final control selection require the applicable team and authority. A distance alone cannot establish a universal preemption requirement. Missing survey endpoints block a numerical clearance or storage finding.
