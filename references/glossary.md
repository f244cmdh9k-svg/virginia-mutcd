# Virginia MUTCD Version 11.0 — Glossary

The definitions are paraphrases of the supplied edition. PDF pages are one-based. A provision's specific definition governs its application under §1C.02 ¶02.

| ID | Term | Location | Meaning in the manual | Application |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| G01 | Standard | §1C.01 ¶01A, PDF p. 50; §1D.03 ¶02, p. 69 | Required or prohibited practice, usually signaled by “shall.” A specific Option can modify a Standard. | Apply within the provision's stated scope. For a limited location-specific infeasibility or impracticability, verify agency authority and the documented engineering-study basis before treating a deviation as appropriate. |
| G02 | Guidance | §1C.01 ¶01B, PDF p. 50 | Recommended practice in typical circumstances, usually signaled by “should.” An appropriate deviation can be supported by engineering judgment or study. | Record the recommendation and the basis for any departure. Check for an applicable Option. |
| G03 | Option | §1C.01 ¶01C, PDF p. 50 | An authorized course under stated conditions, usually signaled by “may.” An Option can modify a Standard or Guidance. | Verify the permission's conditions before applying it. |
| G04 | Support | §1C.01 ¶01D, PDF p. 50 | Information about context or application with no mandate, recommendation, or authorization. | Use it to understand the provision and cite any operative language separately. |
| G05 | Engineering judgment | §1C.02 ¶03 item 76, PDF p. 54; §1D.03, pp. 69–70 | An evaluation applying relevant evidence and engineering principles to device design, use, installation, or operation by or under a qualified professional engineer. VDOT policy may require documentation. | Verify the responsible professional and applicable documentation policy. |
| G06 | Engineering study | §1C.02 ¶03 item 77, PDF p. 54; §1D.03, pp. 69–70 | A written analysis and evaluation supporting a device decision, performed by or under a qualified professional engineer with appropriate traffic engineering expertise. | Track the study as a distinct deliverable wherever the provision requires it. |
| G07 | Site roadways open to public travel | §1C.02 ¶03 item 247, PDF p. 63; Introduction ¶¶03–07, p. 3 | Roadways or bikeways on qualifying public or private sites that the public can use without full-time access restrictions. The definition excludes fully access-controlled site roadways, private highway-rail grade crossings, and parking areas including their driving aisles. | Determine what part of the property the proposal affects and then resolve jurisdiction and adoption. |
| G08 | Traffic control device | §1C.02 ¶03 item 273, PDF p. 65; §1A.02, p. 37 | A sign, signal, marking, channelization device or other device whose primary purpose is to communicate regulation, warning, or guidance to road users. | Confirm that the object and the facility fall within the provision before reviewing device details. |
| G09 | Advisory speed | §1C.02 ¶03 item 08, PDF p. 50; §2C.59, pp. 259–260 | A recommended speed for all vehicles on a highway segment, based on roadway design, operations and conditions. | Use the documented value as the curve-warning input. Record the speed limit separately. |
| G10 | 85th-percentile speed | §1C.02 ¶03 item 249(c), PDF p. 63; §§2C.06 and 3B.12, pp. 211–212 and 648–649 | Speed at or below which 85 percent of the motor vehicles travel. | Keep the observed statistic, posted or statutory limit, and design speed distinct. Use the controlling provision's specified input or formula. |
| G11 | AADT and ADT | §1C.03 ¶01 items 1 and 7, PDF p. 66 | Annual average daily traffic and average daily traffic, respectively. | Preserve the specified averaging period when comparing eligibility or warrant data. |
| G12 | Retroreflectivity | §1C.02 ¶03 item 201, PDF p. 61; §§2A.22 and 3A.05, pp. 101–102 and 619–620 | The property of a surface that returns much of the incident light toward its source. | Review signs and longitudinal markings under their separate measurement and maintenance provisions. |
| G13 | Signal warrant | §4C.01 ¶¶02–03a, PDF p. 735; §§4C.02–4C.10, pp. 737–750 | One of nine specified traffic, safety, school, rail, or roadway-network criteria to screen the need for a conventional traffic control signal. | Under the Virginia provisions, record both a met warrant and separate installation justification for a new signal. |
| G14 | Accessible pedestrian signal | §1C.02 ¶03 item 01, PDF p. 50; Chapter 4K, pp. 818–822 | A device communicating pedestrian signal timing nonvisually, for example through audible messages or vibrating surfaces. | Review accessibility features and detector location alongside pedestrian phasing. |
| G15 | Driving automation system and ADS | §5A.03 ¶01 items B, E and F and Table 5A-1, PDF p. 848 | A driving automation system sustains some or all of the real-time driving task at Levels 1–5. Automated driving system (ADS) denotes a Levels 3–5 system capable of sustaining the entire dynamic driving task. | Establish the actual feature and level before associating a device issue with vehicle automation. |
| G16 | Operational design domain (ODD) | §5A.03 ¶01 item G, PDF p. 848 | The designed operating conditions of a driving automation system or feature, such as road, location, weather or time constraints. | Describe the conditions of an observed device issue. Device uniformity is evidence about the roadway, and the ODD describes the system's design envelope. |
| G17 | Virginia Work Area Protection Manual (VWAPM) | Introduction ¶02, PDF p. 3; §§1A.04 and 1C.03, pp. 38 and 67; Part 6, p. 853 | The separately published Virginia manual that supplies the state's temporary traffic control provisions. | Resolve the applicable authority and obtain the governing edition before reviewing a work-zone layout. |
| G18 | School route plan and school zone | §7A.02 ¶¶01–02 and Figure 7A-1, PDF p. 855; §1C.02 ¶03 item 211, p. 61 | A school route plan maps streets, the school, existing controls, established walk routes and crossings. A school zone refers to a designated roadway segment associated with a school or school activity. | Use the route plan as the basis of the school traffic control plan and assess any school speed limit under §7B.05. |
| G19 | Active and passive grade crossings | §1C.02 ¶03 items 03 and 160, PDF pp. 50 and 59; §§8A.03–8A.05, pp. 874–875 | Active crossings have automatic traffic control triggered by approaching rail traffic. Passive crossings use signs, pavement markings, or both. | Identify existing control before selecting crossing provisions. |
| G20 | Diagnostic Team | §1C.02 ¶03 item 61, PDF p. 53; §§8A.01 and 8A.05, pp. 873 and 875 | Representatives with relevant knowledge from the parties interested in the crossing, including the highway agency, applicable regulator and railroad or transit operator. | Use the team for the engineering study and recommendation on the appropriate traffic control system. |
| G21 | Minimum track clearance distance and clear storage distance | §1C.02 ¶03 items 138 and 39, PDF pp. 58 and 52; §8A.07, p. 876 | Track clearance distance is the roadway span where a vehicle can be struck by rail traffic. Clear storage distance is the room available downstream before the highway intersection's control line or stopping point. | Use §8A.07 for precise endpoints and §1C.02 for skew or exit-gate geometry. Record storage by lane and compare it with the design vehicle and expected queues. |
| G22 | Preemption | §1C.02 ¶03 item 179, PDF p. 60; §8D.09, pp. 910–912 | Transfer of a signal or hybrid beacon from normal operation to a special control mode. | In rail cases, evaluate controller and rail-system interplay and track-clearance timing jointly. |
| G23 | Bicycle box and two-stage bicycle turn box | §1C.02 ¶03 items 23 and 279, PDF pp. 51 and 65; §§9E.11–9E.12, pp. 990–994 | A bicycle box provides a visible red-phase waiting space before stopped motorists at a signalized approach. A two-stage turn box provides a waiting area to complete a direction change at an intersection. | Select the design, markings and signs for the intended maneuver. |
